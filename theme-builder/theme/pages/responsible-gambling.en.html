Recognising problem gambling

Problem gambling develops gradually. Warning signs include:

  • Spending more money on gambling than you can afford
  • Chasing losses with larger bets
  • Lying to family or friends about gambling activity
  • Using gambling to escape stress, anxiety or depression
  • Neglecting work, studies or relationships because of gambling
  • Borrowing money or selling possessions to fund gambling

If several of these describe your situation, professional help may be beneficial.

Tips for safe gambling

Follow these guidelines to keep gambling as entertainment rather than a problem:

  • Set a budget before you start and stick to it
  • Only gamble with money you can afford to lose
  • Set time limits for your sessions
  • Never chase losses by increasing your stakes
  • Avoid gambling when tired, stressed or under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Treat gambling as a form of paid entertainment, not income

Getting professional help

If gambling has become a problem, professional support services are available. These organisations offer free, confidential assistance:

  • BZgA – Federal Centre for Health Education (Germany)
  • GamCare – support and counselling for problem gamblers
  • Gambling Therapy – international online support service
  • Local counselling centres and helplines in your region

Support for family & friends

Problem gambling affects more than just the player. Family members and friends often experience financial stress, relationship issues and emotional strain.

If someone you care about has a gambling problem:

  • Talk openly and without judgement about your concerns
  • Set clear boundaries around shared finances
  • Seek support for yourself from counselling services
  • Avoid enabling the behaviour by lending money or covering debts

Resources such as BZgA provide support for family members affected by someone else’s gambling.
